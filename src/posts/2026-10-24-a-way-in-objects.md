_Forgotten Industries // Reader's Note // Entry 008_

If you found this from an application for writing about collectibles and their history, start here.

The general door is **[A Way In](/a-way-in/)**. This one is narrower. Forgotten Industries has been, since 2014, an archive of objects that outlived the moment they were made for: boutique hardware, recovered machines, and the records that prove what they were. Every piece below asks the questions a collector asks. What is this, exactly? Where has it been? How do we know?

**[Entry 007 - Against Forgetting](/posts/2026-10-01-against-forgetting.html)** - a CaseLabs case bought around 2015, sealed in storage for a decade, and opened again not to build with but to understand. The shortest statement of why an object's history matters more than its spec sheet.

**[CaseLabs Mercury S8 Restoration](/projects/caselabs-mercury-s8/)** - long-form documentation of a boutique case from a maker that no longer exists, written as it was taken apart and inventoried, so someone who has never owned one can follow every part back to its place.

**[X99 Recovery Dossier](/projects/x99-impact-recovery/)** - the biography of a single machine: which board, which processor, confirmed how, and operating since when. Provenance assembled from social-media records, physical intake, and testimony, with every claim marked by how it was verified.

**[Entry 001 - Perspective, Peregrines, & Pang](/posts/2026-06-10-perspective-peregrines-and-pang.html)** - the working vocabulary behind all of it, including _pang_: the moment an abandoned object becomes evidence again.

**[Entry 000 - A Thing Documented Is a Thing Not Yet Lost](/posts/2026-06-06-prelude-a-thing-documented-is-a-thing-not-yet-lost.html)** - the founding entry. Why the archive exists, and proof the voice holds for the length of a full piece.

The common skill is the one collectibles writing depends on: take an object most people would walk past, establish what it is and where it came from, and tell that history so a reader who was never there cares how it ends.

-- Forgotten Industries // Reader's Note // Entry 008
