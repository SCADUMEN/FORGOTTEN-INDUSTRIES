There is a yellow-crowned night heron nest in my neighborhood.

There is at least one juvenile: brown and heavily streaked, pale-spotted across the wings, with a dark bill, a red-orange eye, and the unfinished geometry of a bird that has become large before becoming entirely certain what to do with its body.

It was standing on top of my stepfather’s car.

I drove up calmly.

“Hello, bird.”

The bird remained.

“Look at this fucking bird.”

The bird continued to remain.

I started getting out of the car. The juvenile stretched upward, became extremely vertical, and assumed the deeply unserious posture of an animal confronted with a situation for which no inherited protocol appeared immediately available.

“Yeah,” I told it. “I don’t know what you’re gonna do either.”

We stared at one another for more than thirty seconds.

Neither party appeared to possess a plan.

## 01. ENCOUNTER

The photographs show the sequence.

First, the juvenile occupies the automobile beneath the archway, almost exactly at human eye level. Not hidden in a tree. Not reduced to a silhouette. Present. Close enough to see the eye, the plumage, the neck changing shape.

Close enough for two animals to become mutually aware that the other is watching.

Then the bird ascends.

From the car to the roofline. From eye level to higher ground. From encounter to observation.

A machine becomes a hill. A gutter becomes a shoreline. A roof becomes a cliff.

The animal accepts the available geometry.

It rises.

It looks again.

## 02. TEMPORAL ALTITUDE

Earlier that night, I realized that patience provides perspective in the same way vertical height provides perspective.

Vertical distance changes the relationship between objects in space. Temporal distance changes the relationship between events in memory.

From the ground, the road disappears behind the house. From above, it joins another road. Boundaries become visible. Distances clarify. Things that appeared isolated reveal themselves as parts of systems.

Time does the same thing.

There are decisions that cannot be understood while standing inside them. There are losses that appear total only because the horizon is still too close. There are abandoned objects, interrupted educations, failed machines, wounded bodies, and surviving versions of ourselves whose actual relationship cannot yet be seen from the moment of impact.

The world may not change.

But we move.

The peregrine converts height into force. The drone becomes a pocket-sized hill. The correct angle makes the lost object visible again.

The heron offers the complementary instruction:

Do not always convert height into speed.

Sometimes altitude must be held. Sometimes the field must be observed before it is crossed. Sometimes the correct action is not yet visible because not enough time has passed.

**Patience is temporal altitude.**

## 03. THE BIOLOGIST

I am a biologist.

Bachelor of Science.

For a long time, I treated that degree as one stage in a longer sequence: a prerequisite, a platform, evidence to be submitted to another institution.

But a B.S. in Biological Sciences is not an unfinished credential.

It is a formal record that I spent years learning how life organizes itself.

Cells maintain gradients. Bodies compensate. Tissues remember force. Populations change. Organisms perceive their environments and make decisions within the limits of anatomy, inheritance, experience, and available information.

A tree is not scenery.

A bird is not decoration.

The yard is not empty merely because a human project is not occurring within it.

Everything is alive and breathing.

Not metaphorically.

Respiration. Gas exchange. Circulation. Metabolism. Signaling. Repair. Growth. Senescence. Alarm. Attention.

> _Every living thing is maintaining an impossible arrangement against entropy for as long as it can._

The juvenile on the car was not a symbol first.

It was an animal.

That is what permits the symbolism to mean anything.

I could see its plumage and posture. I could recognize that it was young. I could observe uncertainty without pretending to know its exact internal state. I could laugh because the encounter was genuinely funny.

> _But biology has never required dignity to exclude absurdity._

“Yeah, I don’t know what you’re gonna do either.”

That may be the most honest possible description of two living systems meeting.

Neither organism had complete information.

Both remained attentive.

Both adjusted.

## 04. OMEN

There does not appear to be a securely documented ancient omen tradition belonging specifically to the yellow-crowned night heron.

Good.

We do not need to counterfeit one.

An omen can be understood not as a supernatural prediction, but as an encounter that becomes legible at the exact moment one is prepared to receive its instruction.

The instruction is derived from the animal itself.

The heron waits.

The heron watches.

The heron uses the available elevation.

The heron does not confuse stillness with surrender.

The juvenile is not yet wearing the clean gray plumage, black-and-white face, and pale crown of the adult. It is streaked and mottled. Its shape is correct but unfinished. Its movements contain both competence and comedy.

It does not apologize for being juvenile.

It does not postpone its life until the adult plumage arrives.

It stands where it can stand.

It watches from the height currently available.

Then it moves upward.

So the omen, if I am permitted one, is this:

> Do not despise the unfinished form.  
> Do not move merely to prove that you are moving.  
> Hold the position long enough to understand the field.  
> Use the structure available to you.  
> Gain height.  
> Gain time.  
> Look again.

## 05. SEQUENCE

I have been thinking about birds for weeks.

My aircraft were named **PEREGRINE**.

There is a hawk in the yard.

There is a yellow-crowned night heron nest in the neighborhood.

There is a juvenile standing on the family automobile, looking at me as though I have arrived unexpectedly in its field report.

It would be easy to force this into mythology.

That would violate the method.

The bird is a bird. The nest is a nest. The hawk is a hawk. The drones were consumer aircraft named by a human being who has loved peregrine falcons since childhood.

No supernatural conclusion is required.

And yet meaning does not become fraudulent merely because it arises through attention.

A constellation is not a physical line between stars. The line is made by the observer. The stars remain real.

The peregrine teaches the conversion of perspective into action.

The heron teaches the preservation of perspective before action.

These are not contradictory doctrines.

## _They are sequence._

First: the heron on the automobile.

Then: eye contact.

Then: uncertainty.

Then: laughter.

Then: ascent.

Then: the roofline.

Then: the neighborhood seen from above.

Not revelation.

Not prophecy.

Observation.

A young animal moved through a human landscape and selected successive platforms from which to see.

A biologist arrived home and noticed.

That is enough.

More than enough.

The degree taught me how much is happening.

Patience allows me to see it.
